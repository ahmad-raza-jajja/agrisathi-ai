TITLE: Potato and onion – water, diseases and storage
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general horticulture guidance; verify with provincial horticulture research institutes / extension office and replace with official PDFs)

Potato: needs even soil moisture, especially during tuber initiation and bulking. Very dry spells followed by heavy irrigation can cause cracked or misshapen tubers. Late blight appears as water-soaked dark patches on leaves and stems, with white fuzz underneath in humid cool weather, and can destroy a field quickly; early blight shows concentric ring spots on older leaves. Yellowing of lower leaves can also be natural near maturity, or from nitrogen shortage, water stress or disease. Hill up soil so tubers are covered and do not turn green. Stop irrigation some days before harvest so skins set, and handle tubers gently.

Onion: needs steady moisture during bulb formation, and less water near maturity. Purple blotch shows as purple-centred spots on leaves; thrips cause silvery streaks and leaf tip drying, especially in hot dry weather. Yellow tips can also come from water stress, nitrogen shortage or salinity. After harvest, cure onions in a shaded, well-ventilated place until the necks are tight and dry, then store them in ventilated bins or sacks to cut rot and sprouting.

Both crops lose value fast when stored wet or damaged. Sort out cut, rotten or green tubers and bulbs before storing, keep them cool, dark and ventilated, and check regularly for rot.
