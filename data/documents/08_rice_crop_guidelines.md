TITLE: Rice – water use, nutrient problems and common diseases
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general rice extension guidance; verify with Rice Research Institute / provincial Agriculture Department and replace with official PDFs)

Rice uses much more water than most crops, but not all of it must stand in the field all the time. Alternate wetting and drying (AWD) is a water-saving practice where the field is allowed to dry for some days between irrigations after the plants have established, then flooded again. It can reduce irrigation water in many fields without a large yield loss when done correctly, but keep the field flooded around flowering because water stress then hurts yield. Check local extension advice before starting AWD.

Common causes of yellow or rusty leaves in rice: nitrogen shortage gives pale yellow older leaves and thin plants; zinc deficiency (often called khaira disease) shows as rusty brown spots and stunting in young transplanted rice, more common in alkaline or calcareous soils; iron deficiency shows as yellowing of new leaves in dry-seeded or aerobic fields; salinity shows as white leaf tips and stunting.

Diseases and pests to watch for: blast (spindle-shaped spots with grey centres on leaves, neck rot at heading), bacterial leaf blight (yellow to white stripes starting at leaf tips and edges, often after storms), stem borer (dead central shoot in the young crop, white empty panicles later), and leaf folder. Balanced nitrogen use helps because too much nitrogen increases disease pressure.

Practical checks: look at the age of the crop, which leaves are yellow, whether the problem began after transplanting, soil type, water depth, last fertiliser, and any spots or stripes on leaves. Keep the water depth shallow in early growth, and drain and re-irrigate according to the local plan.
