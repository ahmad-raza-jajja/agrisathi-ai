TITLE: Using weather data for farm decisions
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general agro-meteorology practice; verify with local meteorological department advisories)

Weather data supports decisions but does not replace field observation. Look at three things: recent rainfall, forecast rainfall, and temperature extremes.

If the forecast shows meaningful rain within two to three days, consider delaying irrigation to save water. If it shows no rain and soil is dry at the root depth, irrigation is more justified. Avoid spraying pesticides or foliar fertiliser when rain or strong wind is forecast within a day, and avoid spraying in midday heat.

Hot, dry days increase crop water demand and can cause temporary leaf yellowing, tip burn and leaf rolling, especially in crops already short of water. Cool, humid weather with dew or fog favours foliar fungal diseases such as rusts, so scout fields more often during such spells.

Frost and cold waves can damage tender crops and flowering. Light irrigation before a cold night can sometimes reduce frost damage in some crops, but advice differs by crop, so confirm with local extension staff.

Weather data is a point forecast for coordinates, not for a specific field. Local conditions on your farm can differ.
