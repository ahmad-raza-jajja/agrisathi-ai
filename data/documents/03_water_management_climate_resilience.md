TITLE: Water management and climate resilience on small farms
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general climate-smart agriculture guidance; verify with FAO climate-smart agriculture resources and local extension services)

Hot spells, delayed or heavy rain, and shrinking water supply are the main climate risks for small farms in hot, dry regions. Practical steps lower the risk without large investment.

Heat stress is most damaging to wheat during flowering and grain filling, when very high temperatures can shrink grains. Sowing within the recommended window for the region and choosing a variety suited to the local area can help the crop finish before the hottest weather. Late sowing raises the chance of terminal heat.

To use scarce water better: prefer drip or sprinkler irrigation for vegetables and orchards where affordable; use furrow or bed irrigation rather than flooding wide basins; keep soil covered with residue or mulch; add organic matter such as farmyard manure or compost to raise water-holding capacity; and avoid leaving fields bare and weedy.

Rainwater and drainage: small bunds, ponds or channels can store runoff for a supplementary irrigation; at the same time good drainage prevents waterlogging after heavy rain. Where groundwater is pumped, avoid running tubewells for long hours at midday and maintain pumps and pipes to cut leakage and energy cost.

Plan around forecasts. Check the weekly weather forecast before irrigating or spraying: skip irrigation if good rain is expected, and avoid spraying before rain or in strong wind. Keep an eye on early-warning messages from local meteorological and agriculture departments.

Diversify where possible: growing more than one crop, or including a drought-tolerant crop, spreads risk. Record planting date, variety, irrigation dates and problems each season; these simple records make advice much more specific the next time.
