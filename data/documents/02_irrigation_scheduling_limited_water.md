TITLE: Irrigation scheduling and limited-water strategies for field crops
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general irrigation guidance for canal/tubewell farms; verify with FAO irrigation manuals and provincial Irrigation / Agriculture departments)

When water is limited, the goal is to give water at the growth stages where the crop is most sensitive and to avoid losses, instead of spreading a little water evenly.

For wheat, the stages most sensitive to water shortage are crown root initiation (about three to four weeks after sowing), tillering, jointing, flowering and early grain filling. If only a few irrigations are possible, protect these stages first. Skipping late-season irrigations or the pre-sowing irrigation is usually less harmful than missing CRI or flowering, but the effect depends on soil, weather and variety.

Ways to save water: level the field (laser land levelling where available) so water spreads evenly; use shorter furrows or border strips and smaller basins; irrigate in the cooler part of the day to reduce evaporation; cut off the flow once the water reaches the end of the field; repair and line leaking watercourse sections and field channels; remove weeds that compete for water; and use bed or furrow planting where suitable. Mulch and residue cover reduce evaporation, especially in vegetables.

Check soil moisture before irrigating: dig 10–15 cm and squeeze soil in your hand. If it forms a ball that holds shape, moisture is usually still adequate for many soils; if it crumbles and will not hold shape, irrigation may be due. Avoid over-irrigation because waterlogging reduces root health and can waste nitrogen.

If irrigation water is salty, mix with better water if possible, avoid irrigating in the hottest hours, and ask a local laboratory to test the water. Plan irrigation turns (for example canal rotation, or warabandi where it is applied) so that critical stages match available turns, and keep a simple record of dates and durations.
