TITLE: Cotton – common problems, water needs and nutrition
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general cotton extension guidance; verify with Pakistan Central Cotton Committee / provincial Agriculture Department and replace with official PDFs)

Cotton is sensitive to water stress at flowering and boll formation, and to heat, pests and virus diseases. Yellow, red or curled leaves in cotton have several possible factors, so the pattern must be checked before any spray.

Possible causes of yellowing or discolouration: nitrogen shortage gives pale yellow older leaves; potassium shortage gives yellowing and browning at leaf edges, often in later growth stages; magnesium shortage gives reddish or purple-red leaves with green veins late in the season; waterlogging or irrigation delay gives yellow, wilting plants; salinity gives stunted plants with burnt leaf margins.

Sucking pests such as whitefly, jassid (leafhopper), thrips and aphids cause yellowing, curling, sticky honeydew and sooty mould. Whitefly also spreads cotton leaf curl virus, which shows as upward or downward curling of leaves, thickened veins and small leaf-like outgrowths under leaves, and stunted plants. Check the underside of leaves for insects early in the morning.

Water: avoid long dry gaps from squaring through flowering and boll development; irrigate lightly and more evenly rather than rarely and heavily. Waterlogging after heavy irrigation can cause flower and boll shedding. In very hot weather, flower and boll shedding can also happen from heat stress.

Practical checks: look at which leaves are affected, check the underside of leaves for whitefly or jassid, look for curled leaves and thick veins, check soil moisture at 10–15 cm, and note the date of the last fertiliser and irrigation. Scout fields weekly and compare the pest count with the local economic threshold advised by the extension office before spraying. Spray only when advised, and rotate chemicals as advised to reduce resistance.
