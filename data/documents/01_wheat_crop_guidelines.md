TITLE: Wheat – growth stages, nutrition and common causes of leaf yellowing
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general wheat extension guidance; verify with local Agriculture Extension / PARC and replace with official PDFs)

Wheat passes through germination, crown root initiation (CRI), tillering, jointing (stem elongation), booting and heading, flowering, milk stage, dough stage and maturity. Water or nutrient stress hurts yield most at CRI, tillering, jointing, flowering and grain filling.

Leaf yellowing (chlorosis) in wheat has several possible factors and cannot be diagnosed from yellow leaves alone. Check the pattern first. Uniform yellowing that starts on older, lower leaves and moves upward often points to nitrogen deficiency, especially after heavy irrigation or rain on light soil, or when the nitrogen dose was low or delayed. Yellowing of the youngest leaves can point to sulphur or iron deficiency; interveinal yellowing on new leaves is often linked to iron or zinc problems, more common in alkaline or calcareous soils.

Yellowing in patches where water stands may indicate waterlogging or poor drainage, because roots lack oxygen. Yellowing with dry, rolled or tip-burned leaves in a hot dry spell may indicate drought stress. White or brown leaf tips and stunting in some fields may indicate salinity or sodicity of soil or irrigation water.

Yellow rust shows as narrow yellow-orange stripes of powdery pustules along the leaf veins, and yellow powder rubs off on fingers. It is favoured by cool, humid weather. Aphids cause yellowing with sticky honeydew and curled leaves. Herbicide injury shows yellowing or distortion some days after a spray, often in strips matching the spray pass.

Useful field checks: look at which leaves are affected (old or young), whether yellowing is uniform or in patches, whether stripes or powder are present, soil moisture at 10–15 cm depth, the date and amount of the last nitrogen and irrigation, any recent spray, and the soil or water test result if available. If rust or heavy pest pressure is suspected, show fresh leaf samples to the nearest agriculture extension office before spraying anything.
