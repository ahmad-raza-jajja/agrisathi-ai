TITLE: Water efficiency for vegetables and horticulture crops
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general horticulture irrigation guidance; verify with provincial horticulture research institutes and FAO resources)

Vegetables have shallow roots and are sensitive to both water stress and excess. Irregular watering causes problems such as blossom-end rot and fruit cracking in tomato.

Key practices: use drip or small-furrow irrigation to wet only the root zone; apply mulch (straw or plastic) to reduce evaporation and weeds; water more often with smaller amounts on light sandy soils and less often with larger amounts on heavy soils; water early morning or evening; and keep watering steady during flowering and fruit set, which are the most sensitive stages.

Check moisture by hand at 10–15 cm depth. Wilting in the afternoon that recovers at night can be normal heat stress, but wilting that continues in the morning suggests real water shortage or root problems.

Fertiliser and water work together: nitrogen applied just before heavy irrigation can leach out on light soil, so split doses and avoid applying right before a large irrigation or heavy rain.
