TITLE: Reducing post-harvest loss and handling surplus fresh produce (tomato focus)
SOURCE: AgriSathi starter note (summary of general post-harvest handling guidance; verify with FAO post-harvest loss resources and local horticulture / marketing departments)

Fresh tomatoes are perishable. Losses come from rough handling, heat, sun exposure, deep stacking in crates or bags, and delay before sale. Quality and ripeness stage decide the best option, so sort first.

Step 1 – Sort and grade: separate firm, healthy fruit from soft, cracked or damaged fruit, and separate by ripeness (green-mature, turning, fully red). Remove rotten fruit so it does not spoil the rest.

Step 2 – Slow the spoilage: keep produce in shade, handle gently, use shallow ventilated crates instead of deep sacks, and keep it cool and well ventilated. Mature green or turning tomatoes keep longer than fully ripe ones; fully ripe fruit may only hold for a few days in hot weather.

Step 3 – Options for the good fruit: sell in a different market or to a different buyer than the usual one (a nearby wholesale market, a retailer, a restaurant or hotel, a processor); sell directly to consumers through a roadside stall or group delivery; or wait a short time if fruit is not ripe and prices may recover, but only if storage conditions allow it.

Step 4 – Processing for surplus ripe fruit: tomatoes can be processed into pulp, puree, paste, sauce or dried tomato. Small processing needs clean equipment, safe heat treatment and clean containers; follow hygiene guidance from the local food authority before selling processed products. Sun drying needs clean, covered drying trays.

Step 5 – Damaged but still edible fruit: sell at a reduced price to processors or street vendors, donate to a local food bank, community kitchen or charity, or use as animal feed for fruit that is not fit for people. Compost fruit that is rotten.

This is decision support only. Check current prices at the local market and contact buyers yourself; no buyer is guaranteed.
